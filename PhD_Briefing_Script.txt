================================================================================
                    5-MINUTE PhD BRIEFING SCRIPT
                   B2Z1 Autonomous Grasping System
================================================================================

SLIDE 1: TITLE PAGE (30 seconds)
─────────────────────────────────
[Show title slide - make eye contact]

"Thank you for your time. Today I want to brief you on my thesis progress.
I'm working on autonomous grasping for the B2Z1 quadruped-arm system.
The approach is straightforward: using GraspNet for visual grasping 
and reinforcement learning for execution strategy."

[Pause 2 seconds, let it sink in]


SLIDE 2: PROBLEM (1 minute)
───────────────────────────
[Click to reveal problem]

"The challenge is significant. A B2Z1 has 18 degrees of freedom - 
12 for the legs, 6 for the arm, plus the gripper. That's a complex control space.

When we try to learn grasping from scratch using pure RL, 
the success rate is only around 45 percent. Very low.

The reason is sample efficiency - the system needs too many interactions 
to discover good behaviors.

So the key question is: How can we learn grasping efficiently 
on such a complex system?"


SLIDE 3: OUR APPROACH (1 minute 30 seconds)
────────────────────────────────────────────
[Click to reveal components]

"Our solution has two main components.

First: Visual Grasping using GraspNet-Baseline.
This is a pre-trained CNN from a CVPR 2020 paper. 
It's already trained on massive grasp datasets, 
so it can generate good grasp candidates - around 50 of them - 
with 80 to 85 percent accuracy. 

The key point: We don't retrain this. It's frozen. 
We just use it as-is.

Second: Execution Strategy using PPO, a standard RL algorithm.
After GraspNet suggests a grasp, our RL policy learns 
how to actually execute that grasp on the B2Z1.
We only need to train this for 10K iterations.

By combining these two approaches, we expect to achieve 
around 75 to 80 percent success rate. That's a significant improvement 
from the baseline 45 percent."


SLIDE 4: TIMELINE & PROGRESS (1 minute 30 seconds)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
[Click to show progress]

"Let me walk you through the timeline.

Phase 1 is already complete. We've trained B2Z1's base locomotion 
for 50K iterations, and the model checkpoint is saved.

Now we're starting Phase 2, which has three parts:

Week 1: We integrate GraspNet with the Isaac Gym environment. 
This takes about 7 days - testing, debugging, smoke tests.

Week 2-3: We train the RL policy for 10K iterations. 
This is the core training phase. We expect to see the success rate 
climbing from zero to our target of 75-80 percent.

Week 4: We write the final paper - 8 to 10 pages. 
This includes methods, results, figures, discussion.

All of this fits within a 4-week deadline."


SLIDE 5: SUMMARY (1 minute)
──────────────────────────
[Show summary]

"To summarize why this work matters:

First, this is the first multi-component grasping system 
deployed on B2Z1. It's not trivial.

Second, we're leveraging a pre-trained GraspNet from CVPR 2020, 
which is a clear, published baseline. We're not inventing the wheel - 
we're using existing, proven technology.

Third, the timeline is aggressive but realistic. 
4 weeks total, including the paper.

Our next milestones are:

End of Week 1: We'll have smoke test results showing 
the system runs without crashes.

End of Week 2: We'll show initial success examples - 
actual videos of the robot grasping objects.

End of Week 4: The paper is submitted.

[Pause, make eye contact]

Do you have any questions about this approach?"

                          QUICK REFERENCE NOTES

Key Numbers to Remember:
- 18 DOF (control space complexity)
- 45% (RL-only baseline)
- 80-85% (GraspNet accuracy)
- 75-80% (our expected result)
- 4 weeks (total timeline)
- 10K iterations (RL training)
- 50 grasp candidates (per inference)

If Advisor Asks...

Q: "Why GraspNet and not something else?"
A: "GraspNet is from CVPR 2020 with published benchmarks. 
   It's open-source with pre-trained models. No licensing issues.
   This makes it ideal for a 4-week project."

Q: "Can you really achieve 75-80% success rate?"
A: "GraspNet is already 80-85% accurate. 
   RL then learns the execution refinement - 
   that usually adds 5-10% when starting from a good grasp candidate.
   So yes, this is a conservative estimate."

Q: "What about the sim-to-real gap?"
A: "Good question. For this thesis, we're validating in simulation.
   If the advisor approves, real robot experiments could be next work."

Q: "Is 4 weeks realistic?"
A: "Phase 1 is done. Phase 2 has clear milestones and a day-by-day checklist.
   The biggest risk is integration bugs, but we've prepared 
   all the framework code in advance."

================================================================================
                          BODY LANGUAGE TIPS
================================================================================

- Open posture, hands visible
- Make eye contact with advisor frequently
- Speak clearly, not too fast (most people speak too fast when nervous)
- Pause after showing a slide (let them read it)
- Use hand gestures to emphasize numbers ("75 to 80 percent")
- Smile when discussing results

Time management:
- Opening: 30 seconds
- Problem: 1 minute (watch the time - don't linger)
- Solution: 1.5 minutes (this is the core, spend time here)
- Timeline: 1.5 minutes (advisor will want details)
- Summary: 1 minute
- Total: 5 minutes + buffer for questions

If running out of time:
- Skip the details in "Summary"
- Just say: "We're on track for 4 weeks, with results expected by Week 2."
